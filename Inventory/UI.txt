UI

Your inventory domain is now strong enough that the UI should follow business workflows, not just database CRUD screens.

A good ERP inventory UI is usually divided into these major areas:

```text id="10u6cw"
Warehouse Management
Inventory Operations
Stock Visibility
Transfers
Adjustments
Batch Tracking
Inventory Auditing
Reports
```

# Most Important Screens

If building incrementally, prioritize in this order:

---

# Phase 1 (Core Operations)

Build first:

1. Warehouses
2. Current Stock
3. Stock In
4. Stock Out
5. Transfers
6. Movements

This gives a usable ERP inventory module.

---

# Phase 2 (Operational Maturity)

Add:

* reservations
* adjustments
* batch expiry
* partial receiving

---

# Phase 3 (Enterprise)

Add:

* stock counting
* barcode scanning
* valuation
* dashboards
* analytics

---

# IMPORTANT UX Advice

Inventory systems become unusable if:

* too many popup dialogs
* too many navigation steps
* slow grids
* poor filtering

Your grids must support:

* server-side paging
* fast search
* filtering
* export
* keyboard navigation

Especially:

```text id="g8go9v"
Current Stock
Movements
Transfers
```

These pages become huge in real ERP systems.
